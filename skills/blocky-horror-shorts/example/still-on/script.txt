In our town, every house keeps its porch light on all *night*.
If your light goes out, something walks up your steps and *knocks*.
Whoever answers the door is gone by *morning*.
The taken still try to come home. They just need a light to find the *door*.
So nobody here ever lets a bulb burn out.
Every kid has a light buddy across the street.
At midnight, you flash your flashlight twice. "Still *on*?"
And they flash back twice. "Still on."
Theo lived across from me. For six years, he never missed one *midnight*.
He was the best friend I ever had.
Funny thing is, my bulb never burned out. Not *once*.
Mom still set my plate at dinner. She just never looked at *it*.
At school, nobody sat next to me.
Theo's mom cried every time she walked past our *porch*.
And Theo never looked at me when we talked. He looked at the *light*.
This winter, the storms kept knocking the power out.
In December, there were forty lights on our road. By January, twenty-*two*.
One night, Mrs. Hale's porch went dark next door.
Three knocks. Slow ones.
She opened the door, like she was expecting *someone*.
In the morning, her slippers were on the *mat*.
Then Theo told me his family was moving away.
The night before he left, a storm hit. Every light went out. Even *mine*.
Theo was on my porch in the rain, holding his flashlight up to my door.
At four a.m., something came up the steps.
Three knocks. Slow ones.
I reached for the knob. Theo screamed, "*Don't*!" Not at the thing. At *me*.
"You already answered it," he said. "Six years *ago*."
And I remembered. The blackout. The knocks. My hand on the *knob*.
My bulb never burned out because Theo changed it. Every single *night*.
Because the taken still come home. They just need a *light*.
Theo held that flashlight until sunrise. Then the thing was *gone*.
Theo's truck left that afternoon. On our porch, a crate of bulbs, and his flashlight.
That night, at midnight, I flashed my light twice. Across the street, *nothing*.
Then our porch light flickered on.
Mom was standing under it, holding Theo's flashlight.
She looked right at my window, and flashed it twice.
Still on.
