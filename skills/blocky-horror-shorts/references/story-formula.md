# Story formula

Short twist-only stories ("rule -> instant twist", 20 s) don't hold viewers: nobody is attached to anyone. What works
is an emotional best-friend story of **at most 90 seconds** (about 25-30 lines: compress the clue and countdown beats).

## Premise
Either a supernatural **town rule** stated as fact, or a whimsical-dark **"world where X"** (a world where shadows
clock out at midnight, where every mirror runs one day late...) that hides a twist. Check the premise against popular animated-horror channels'
recent uploads so it isn't a near-copy of a hit from last week.

## Shape
1. **A supernatural town rule, stated as plain fact.** ("Every porch light stays on all night. If yours goes out...")
2. **How the town lives with it:** 3-5 concrete, visual details (crates of spare bulbs, kids' rituals, a sign).
3. **The narrator is the exception** in some way.
4. **A best friend + a ritual phrase** they repeat ("Still on?" / "Same time tomorrow?").
5. **Planted clues** about the friend or the narrator: 3-4 small odd things that only make sense after the twist.
6. **A countdown** to the night it happens (30 -> 21 -> 9 -> 2...).
7. **One witnessed taking:** we see the rule work on someone else.
8. **The last night:** the rule comes for the narrator; the friend acts.
9. **The twist that recasts the friend** (and every clue) - e.g. the narrator was the one taken long ago.
10. **The ending echoes the ritual phrase, cut mid-word** ("Still o-"). Hard cut on the last frame.

## Writing rules
- Original only: invent your own rule, characters and lines; never reuse another creator's story.
- One script line = one shot. Lines of 4-14 words read best. About 25-30 lines, at most 90 s spoken.
- Give every character a distinct look (hair colour and style, outfit, one accessory) so each one reads at a glance.
- Write a **twist rule** for the picture (e.g. "nobody ever looks at or answers the narrator until line 37") and
  hand it to every part agent: breaking it on screen spoils the twist.
- Leading "..." makes some TTS models repeat words; avoid it. Spell numbers the way they should be spoken.
